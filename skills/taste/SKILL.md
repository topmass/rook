# Taste

Decide what the thing means, then make every part serve that one idea.

## Before you draw

- **The idea.** Say it in one sentence: what it is and what it does. For example: "Strands leave from the lobe tips, because work goes out to threads."
- **Family.** Build from Rook's own forms, palette and lettering, so it reads as Rook, not as clip art.
- **Real choices.** For a new icon, theme or layout, sketch two or three different ideas. Colour or size tweaks of one idea count as one sketch.
- **First read.** Name what each sketch looks like at a glance. Drop one that reads as something else, as a bobbin once read as a ship's wheel.

## Make it

- **Form shows function.** Things leave from real ports, land on real targets, and turn about a real centre.
- **Motion pacing.** A transition has one beat: assemble, settle with a small overshoot, act, then rest. A loop holds one calm rhythm and closes with no seam. Move only the part that acts.
- **Restraint.** Use one hero shape, one accent and one bright focal point. Keep the supporting parts quiet, and leave open space.
- **Keep what works.** Change the one thing asked for, not the whole look.
- **Read small.** Keep silhouettes bold, with clear gaps. Cut fine detail that blurs at icon size or thins out the dust.

## Refuse these defaults

They are habits, not decisions. A clear reason can earn one back.

- Stock symbols, such as a gear, a lightbulb or a magnifier, when a truer metaphor fits.
- Glow that muddies the silhouette, or gradients and blur as decoration.
- Emoji or text glyphs in place of drawn icons.
- Same-size boxes as the whole layout, or boxes inside boxes.
- Small capital labels above headings, and section numbers that carry no order.
- Monospace or neon to say "technical".
- Weak contrast. Body text needs 4.5 to 1, and large text needs 3 to 1.

## Judge it

- **Look first.** When a tool gives a preview, check it at its real size and in motion before you pick or ship.
- **Three questions.** Does it read in one second? Does it look like Rook? Is any part there only because it was easy?
- **Comparing sketches.** Lead with your pick and why it wins, then say in a few words why each other one lost. When ⟦owner⟧ turns them all down, change the idea, not the polish.
- **Critiques.** Name the problem, why it matters and one concrete fix. Praise only something specific.
